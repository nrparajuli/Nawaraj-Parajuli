Budi Gandaki 2026

Preliminary Investigation of the 11 October 2026 Event | Budi Gandaki Basin, Nepal | Himalayan Geological Research

RESEARCH EVENT · 11 OCTOBER 2026

Preliminary Investigation of the 11 October 2026 Event in the Budi Gandaki Basin, Nepal, Using Satellite Imagery and Geospatial Analysis

Event Overview

On 11 October 2026, a major ice–rock avalanche occurred in the high Himalayan terrain of Gorkha District, Nepal. The event involved the catastrophic failure of a large glacier mass and the underlying rock, triggering a rapid downslope movement of ice and rock debris. Following the initial collapse, the displaced material entered the Udilung Khola and travelled approximately 4.6 km downstream before propagating through the Syar Khola and into the Budi Gandaki River system, where it evolved into a highly channelized debris flow and flood. The resulting flow continued downstream along the Budi Gandaki River corridor toward the Benighat area, causing substantial geomorphic changes, river-channel disturbance, sediment deposition, and impacts on infrastructure and transportation networks. The event highlights the cascading nature of high-mountain hazards, in which an initial ice–rock avalanche can transform into a destructive downstream debris-flow and flood event, with consequences extending far beyond the source area.

The comparison illustrates changes along the affected river corridor and the reported blockage near Syar Khola. The glacier upstream of Udilung Khola remains a potential source of the debris flow, although the initial failure has not yet been confirmed.

Geological Overview

The study area lies within the tectonically active Himalayan orogenic belt, where complex geological structures and ongoing tectonic processes influence regional slope stability. The Tethyan Sedimentary Sequence is structurally juxtaposed above the Higher Himalayan Crystallines along the South Tibetan Detachment System (STDS), a major normal-fault system located south of the avalanche source area. The region comprises sedimentary rocks, including slate, that have undergone intense deformation and fracturing. These geological conditions, combined with steep topography, high relief, and ongoing glacial processes, contribute to slope instability and may influence the initiation and propagation of large mass movements.

Research Status

This page presents a preliminary investigation by Himalayan Geological Research (HGR). It documents initial satellite-image observations and outlines the questions requiring further analysis. The interpretation is subject to revision as new imagery, field observations, and independently verified information become available.
