RESEARCH EVENT · 11 OCTOBER 2026

Preliminary Investigation of the 11 October 2026 Event in the Budi Gandaki Basin, Nepal, Using Satellite Imagery and Geospatial Analysis

Event Overview

On 11 October 2026, a major ice–rock avalanche occurred in the high Himalayan terrain in Gorkha, Nepal. The event involved the catastrophic collapse of a large glacier mass together with the underlying rock, generating a massive downslope movement. Following the initial failure, the displaced material entered the Udilung Khola, and after travelling about 4.6 km downstream in Syar Khola and and Budi Gandaki Nadi, where it transformed into a highly channelized debris flow and flood. The flow propagated downstream along the Budi Gandaki Nadi corridor toward the Benighat area, resulting in extensive geomorphic changes, river-channel disturbance, sediment deposition, and impacts on infrastructure and transportation. The event represents a significant high-mountain cascading hazard in which an ice–rock avalanche evolved into a large downstream debris-flow and flood event with transboundary consequences.

The comparison illustrates changes along the affected river corridor and the reported blockage near Syar Khola. The glacier upstream of Udilung Khola remains a potential source of the debris flow, although the initial failure has not yet been confirmed.

Geological Overview

The study area lies within the tectonically active Himalayan orogenic belt. The Tethyan Sedimentary Sequence overlies the Higher Himlayan Crystallines and along the South Tibetan Detachment System (STDS) zone north of the avalanche source area. The (STDS) contact is normal fault. The region comprises sedimentary rocks, including slates affected by intense deformation and fracturing. Steep, high-relief terrain and glacial processes further contribute to slope instability.

Research Status

This page presents a preliminary investigation by Himalayan Geological Research (HGR). It documents initial satellite-image observations and outlines the questions requiring further analysis. The interpretation is subject to revision as new imagery, field observations, and independently verified information become available.
