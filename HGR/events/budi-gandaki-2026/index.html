Ice–Rock Avalanche and Associated Debris-Flow Event | Gorkha, Nepal | Himalayan Geological Research

RESEARCH EVENT · OCTOBER 2026

October 2026 Shringri Himal Ice–Rock Avalanche and Budi Gandaki Debris-Flow Event, Gorkha, Nepal: Preliminary Source Identification Using Satellite Imagery

Event Overview

On 11 October 2026, at approximately 4:00 a.m. Nepal Standard Time, a major ice–rock avalanche occurred in the high Himalayan terrain near Shringri Himal in Gorkha District, Nepal. The event involved the failure of an ice and rock mass, generating a rapid downslope movement into the Udilung Khola drainage system. The displaced material subsequently propagated downstream through the Shyar Khola and into the Budi Gandaki River, where it developed into a debris-flow and flood event. The resulting flow caused geomorphic changes along the affected river corridor, including channel disturbance, sediment mobilization and deposition, with impacts extending downstream. Satellite imagery acquired on 6 October (pre-event) and 13 October 2026 (post-event) clearly identifies the avalanche source area within the Tethyan Sedimentary Sequence, upstream of Udilung Khola. The event represents a cascading high-mountain hazard in which an ice–rock avalanche initiated a sequence of downstream mass-flow and flood processes within the Budi Gandaki river system.
