RESEARCH EVENT · 11 OCTOBER 2026

Preliminary Investigation of the 11 October 2026 Event in the Budi Gandaki Basin, Nepal, Using Satellite Imagery and Geospatial Analysis

Event Overview

On 11 October 2026, a river-corridor disturbance and associated flooding were reported in the Budi Gandaki basin of Nepal. This preliminary investigation examines satellite imagery acquired on 1 and 11 October 2026 to identify changes along the affected river corridor and investigate the reported blockage near Syar Khola.

A potential glacier-related source upstream of Udilung Khola is being considered as one possible explanation for the reported downstream disturbance. However, cloud cover obscures the relevant glacier area in the available 11 October imagery. The source conditions and any possible glacier or rock-slope failure therefore cannot be directly assessed from this image alone.

The investigation focuses on changes visible in satellite imagery, possible channel disturbance, the reported blockage near Syar Khola, and the potential relationship between the upstream terrain and downstream impacts. The initial failure mechanism and the sequence of processes responsible for the reported event remain unconfirmed.

Satellite-Image Comparison

The image comparison provides an initial basis for investigating changes along the affected river corridor. Particular attention is directed toward the reported blockage near Syar Khola and possible changes in the surrounding river channel and adjacent terrain.

Although the available imagery can help identify landscape changes between the acquisition dates, it does not independently establish the cause of those changes. The cloud-obscured source area limits the assessment of any possible glacier-related failure. Additional imagery and independent observations are required to determine whether an ice–rock avalanche or another process initiated the event.

Preliminary Geomorphic Interpretation

Potential geomorphic indicators of the event include changes in river-channel configuration, possible sediment accumulation, local channel obstruction, and disturbance of the river corridor. These features require systematic comparison with pre-event conditions before their extent and significance can be established.

If a blockage developed along the river, its location, dimensions, composition, and possible influence on downstream flow would be important parameters for further investigation. However, the presence, origin, and dimensions of any blockage must be established from suitable imagery, field evidence, or reliable independent reports.

The available information is not yet sufficient to quantify the source volume, transported sediment volume, peak discharge, flow velocity, or total affected area. These parameters will require additional measurements and appropriate analytical methods.

Geological Overview

The study area lies within the tectonically active Himalayan orogenic belt, where high relief, steep slopes, active deformation, and fluvial and glacial processes can contribute to slope instability and sediment mobilisation. Regional geological conditions may influence the development of slope failures and the subsequent movement of material through confined mountain valleys.

The regional geology includes metamorphic and sedimentary rocks affected by varying degrees of deformation and fracturing. The South Tibetan Detachment System (STDS) is an important regional structural system in the Higher Himalaya. Its specific relationship to the potential source area, however, must be assessed using appropriate geological maps, local structural evidence, and field observations.

No specific fault geometry or structural control on the event is assigned at this preliminary stage. Further geological investigation is needed before relating the reported disturbance to a particular structure or failure mechanism.

Remote Sensing and Geospatial Methods

The investigation will use multi-date satellite imagery to examine changes in the river corridor and surrounding terrain. Where suitable data are available, additional analysis may include image registration, visual change detection, river-channel mapping, elevation-data comparison, and delineation of potentially affected areas.

Digital elevation models and other geospatial datasets may support the reconstruction of the local terrain, assessment of channel gradients, and identification of possible sediment pathways. The reliability of any derived measurements will depend on data resolution, image quality, spatial alignment, and the availability of appropriate pre-event reference data.

Ongoing Investigation

Further investigation will focus on obtaining suitable cloud-free imagery of the potential source area, comparing pre-event and post-event surface conditions, examining possible river-channel changes, and evaluating available elevation data. Independent observations and verified reports will also be important for establishing the event sequence.

The findings will be updated as additional evidence becomes available. Interpretations of the source, triggering mechanism, event magnitude, and downstream sediment transport remain provisional until supported by sufficient evidence.

Research Status

This page presents a preliminary investigation by Himalayan Geological Research (HGR). It documents initial satellite-image observations and outlines the questions requiring further analysis. The interpretation is subject to revision as new imagery, field observations, and independently verified information become available.
