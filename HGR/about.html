About HGR

Himalayan Geological Research (HGR) is a research initiative focused on understanding the geology, geomorphology, natural hazards, and evolving landscapes of the Himalayan region. We bring together field observations, geological investigations, remote sensing, GIS, satellite imagery, drone surveys, and geospatial analysis to document and understand geological processes across the Himalaya.

The Himalaya is a dynamic mountain system where tectonic activity, glacial processes, extreme weather, river erosion, and human development interact to produce rapidly changing landscapes and complex natural hazards. HGR aims to document these processes through field-based research and modern geospatial technologies, with particular emphasis on landslides, rockfalls, ice–rock avalanches, glacial and hydrological hazards, debris flows, floods, and associated cascading processes. Our work combines field evidence with Earth observation and geospatial analysis to reconstruct events, measure their spatial and temporal extent, investigate their mechanisms, and assess their downstream impacts. We also focus on developing accessible datasets, maps, visualizations, and scientific documentation that can support research, education, hazard assessment, and informed decision-making.

Himalayan Geological Research

HGR is committed to strengthening geological research in the Himalaya through evidence-based investigation, open scientific communication, and collaboration among researchers, students, institutions, and local communities. Our goal is not only to understand how Himalayan landscapes change, but also to contribute to a better understanding of the hazards and geological processes that shape life and infrastructure across the mountain region.

Research approach

HGR integrates field investigations, remote sensing, GIS, satellite imagery, drone surveys, and geospatial analysis to understand Himalayan geological processes and natural hazards. We combine field evidence with Earth-observation data to reconstruct events, assess their extent and impacts, and understand how geological hazards evolve across the Himalayan landscape.

Advancing geoscience knowledge through field investigation, remote sensing, geospatial analysis and scientific communication.
